How can we better leverage data for the future using modern technologies such as machine learning (ML) or artificial intelligence (AI)? This is a common challenge I encounter in conversations with these startups, as well as their uncertainty about how to fully take advantage of their data for the future. Even startups that are only a few years old are sitting on an astonishing number of data points that hold the key to providing the foresight necessary for making significantly improved decisions. These companies can utilize their past data to gain insights, drive business decisions, and iterate on their products - this is normally called descriptive analytics. But how can they harness this same data to predict future outcomes? Modern data analytics that uses machine learning and artificial intelligence can help these companies leap ahead of their competitors.

Let's take a closer look at healthcare data as an example. In this field, numerous startups are making impressive progress. However, before delving into it, it's important to note that data in healthcare must be protected and secured in accordance with HIPAA rules. Nonetheless, a significant portion of this data can be put to internal use by key stakeholders for analytics.For instance, historical data can be utilized to predict future patient outcomes, enhance provider decision-making, and improve the diagnosis of patient problems. To illustrate, we can analyze past trends in diagnosing certain patients to evaluate their effectiveness in treating specific diseases or conditions. By leveraging this historical data, we can achieve better diagnostic capabilities.

Let's dive into this exciting world of future analytics! We will look at three distinct types of analytics that businesses use to make data-driven decisions: Comparative analytics, Predictive analytics, and Prescriptive analytics. These approaches each serve a unique purpose and offer valuable insights. In essence, they all fall under the umbrella of data analytics, but they tackle different questions:
Comparative Analytics -  Imagine it as a detective analyzing various datasets to uncover similarities, differences, and emerging trends. It's all about spotting patterns and connections.
Predictive Analytics -  Think of it as your crystal ball for data. Predictive Analytics forecasts what's most likely to happen in the future based on historical data and trends.
Prescriptive Analytics -  This is like having a personal advisor in the data world. It not only predicts future outcomes but also suggests possible consequences based on different choices or actions you might take.
Comparative Analytics
Comparative analytics serves the purpose of evaluating and contrasting various datasets to discern similarities, distinctions, and emerging trends. Its methodology relies on data comparison techniques and benchmarking against industry or peer data to extract valuable insights. The outcomes of comparative analytics reveal how a particular entity or dataset compares to another, shedding light on strengths, weaknesses, and opportunities for improvement.

Practical examples of this approach with an EHR or EMR software solution involve providing valuable comparative insights to the end users of these products, namely the healthcare practices themselves. These insights can be within a practice or in relation to other similar practices. For instance, this could involve comparing a practice's financial performance, such as profitability, to that of similar competitors, evaluating patient satisfaction scores, or conducting an in-depth analysis of a provider's productivity within a practice or in comparison to similar practices in a region.

Predictive Analytics
Predictive analytics revolves around anticipating future possibilities by drawing insights from past and current trends, patterns, and behaviors. By employing techniques such as data mining, statistical modeling, and artificial intelligence, predictive analytics scrutinizes historical and real-time data to provide businesses with forecasts. These forecasts enable clinical practices to manage risks, anticipate patient churn, offer tailored product suggestions, and much more.
This approach can be applied to reducing costs related to patient appointment no-shows and readmission penalties. For a clinical practice, analyzing a patient's past appointment behavior can help predict their future actions, such as late cancellations or no-shows. Predictive analytics can also assist insurance companies in better identifying patients who require interventions to prevent diseases and improve overall health outcomes.

Prescriptive Analytics
Prescriptive analytics, driven by the question "What is the optimal next step?" assimilates data from various sources, employing AI solutions like machine learning algorithms to provide recommendations for achieving predefined outcomes. This approach empowers healthcare decision-makers to optimize business outcomes for patients and providers.
By utilizing prescriptive models, clinical practices, for instance, can gain insights into the potential impact of decisions on patient outcomes, allowing for adjustments to enhance success rates. Additionally, it aids in tailoring therapy programs or medications based on past successes.
Moreover, prescriptive analytics extends its utility to assessing a patient's existing conditions, evaluating their risk for future conditions, and developing targeted preventative treatment plans with those risks in consideration.


In summary, comparative analytics is about comparing data to identify insights, predictive analytics is about predicting future events, and prescriptive analytics is about recommending actions to achieve specific goals. These three types of analytics are often used in combination to provide a comprehensive data-driven decision-making framework for businesses and organizations.
